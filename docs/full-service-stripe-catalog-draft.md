## Pricing model
- A **fixed package** can display its proposed total and deposit, with **Book Now → request date**. Booking confirmation requires owner approval of availability and written terms.
- A **quote-first** service may display "**Starting at $X — Request a Quote**" only if approved by the owner. Do not create a generic $X charge for a custom project.
- Default deposit for these examples: **50%**, with remaining balance due according to the signed agreement. **Before final HD delivery** is a proposed payment milestone, not an already agreed policy.
- Always quote travel, extra time, second shooters, additional editing, licensing, turnaround/rush service, sales tax requirements and special expenses separately as applicable.

## How to enter products in Stripe safely

### Fixed-price packages
Create **one product per approved package** and, where useful, separate one-time prices or clearly named deposit items. Customer-facing descriptions must specify the scope and that a date is not reserved until approved. For example:
- **Basketball Photography — Game Coverage Deposit** — $175 one-time; counts toward the proposed $350 package; requires prior date/terms approval.
- **Basketball Videography — Game Coverage Deposit** — $250 one-time; counts toward the proposed $500 package; video deliverables must be agreed in writing before sending payment.
- For other fixed packages, use the table's 50% amount **only after approval**, and avoid activating or advertising unapproved prices.

### Final balance
Prefer **customer-specific Stripe invoices** for the remaining 50%, referencing the agreed full project price and deposit already received. A generic final-payment link does **not** automatically associate itself with an earlier deposit or reconcile a specific booking. Don't publicly display balance-payment links.

### Quote-first projects
Do **not** create fixed-price payment links or auto-charge a deposit. Send a custom quote/invoice after approval of scope and agreement. You can add Stripe products later if repeated standardized tiers emerge.

### Locked deliverables
Stripe does not protect media files by itself. A future client portal needs protected storage and Stripe-confirmed payment before granting final-file access. Don't expose files through public direct URLs.
